What the margins pay for

Cost of goods runs $133,931 a month against $517,177 of revenue, a blended 74.1% margin. What they cover:

CategoryMarginWhat the cost side is
Recovery80–85%Consumables, filters and water treatment, linens, device servicing contracts. Almost no per-session variable cost, which is why these margins are the highest in the building.
Regenerative72–78%PRP kits and centrifuge consumables, IV bags, vitamins and NAD+, peptides and pharmacy cost, tubing and sharps.
Aesthetics65–70%Neuromodulator and filler units at acquisition cost, laser tips and consumables, RF and cryo applicators, topicals. The lowest wellness margins because product cost dominates.
Diagnostics62–70%Reference lab send-out fees, DEXA consumables, DNA and DUTCH collection kits, interpretation time.
Surgery — facility fee78–80%Disposables, implants, drugs and linens, instrument reprocessing. Clinical staff sits in fixed overhead and the surgeon takes no share of the facility fee, which is why these exceed the treatment-room lines.
Surgery — full fee73–76%As above, plus graft handling and technician time on hair restoration and laser consumables on ablative resurfacing.

Circuit consumables — towels, laundry, water and amenities — are carried separately at $8 a visit across 2,345 monthly visits, or $18,760.

Working capital and debt service reserve — $1,146,000

Sized against the modelled cash trough, not set to a round number.

This is the one capital line that is computed rather than estimated. The model runs a 36-month cash curve and this reserve must exceed the deepest cumulative shortfall; a build assert fails if it does not. That is why the figure is not round.
